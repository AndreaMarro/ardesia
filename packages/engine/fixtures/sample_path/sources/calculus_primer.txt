Calculus Primer (synthetic source — FAKE content for tests)

1. The derivative of a function f at a point x measures the instantaneous
rate of change. It is defined as the limit of the difference quotient
(f(x + h) - f(x)) / h as h approaches zero, when that limit exists.

2. Power rule: the derivative of x^n is n * x^(n-1). The product rule states
that (u*v)' = u'*v + u*v'. The chain rule states that (f(g(x)))' =
f'(g(x)) * g'(x).

3. An integral is the antiderivative: F is an antiderivative of f when
F'(x) = f(x). The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus links the definite
integral of f over [a, b] to F(b) - F(a).
